Dental Fillings

A quick, painless way to repair cavities and restore decayed or damaged teeth — protecting your smile with natural-looking, tooth-coloured fillings.

What Is a Dental Filling?

A dental filling repairs a tooth damaged by decay or a minor fracture. The damaged part is removed and the space is filled with a durable material that restores the tooth's shape, strength, and function.

When a cavity forms, bacteria gradually eat away at the tooth. A filling stops this process by cleaning out the decay and sealing the tooth, preventing further damage and relieving sensitivity.

Today's tooth-coloured composite fillings are matched to the natural shade of your tooth, so repairs are virtually invisible — a strong, discreet way to keep your smile healthy.

Why Treating a Cavity Early Matters

A small cavity won't heal on its own. Treating it early with a simple filling prevents a chain of bigger problems:

  • Stops decay spreading A filling removes and seals the cavity before it grows deeper.
  • Prevents toothache Treating decay early avoids the pain of an infected nerve.
  • Avoids root canals Left untreated, decay can reach the pulp and require a root canal.
  • Saves the tooth Early treatment helps you keep your natural tooth intact.
  • Cheaper and simpler A filling is far quicker and more affordable than treating advanced decay.
1 visit Most fillings are completed in a single, comfortable visit — a fast, painless way to stop decay and protect your tooth for years.

Tooth-Coloured vs. Metal Fillings

Fillings come in different materials. Here's how modern tooth-coloured composite compares with traditional metal amalgam:

Feature Metal (Amalgam) Tooth-Coloured (Composite)
Appearance Silver-grey and clearly visible Matched to your tooth — virtually invisible
Bonding Held in place mechanically Bonds directly to the tooth for a tight seal
Tooth structure More healthy tooth may need removing Preserves more of your natural tooth
Best for Large fillings in back teeth Front and back teeth, especially visible areas
Aesthetics Noticeable when you smile or laugh Blends in seamlessly with your smile

We use natural-looking tooth-coloured fillings in most cases. During your visit, Dr Deepesh Agrawal will recommend the best material for your tooth.

The Filling Procedure, Step by Step

Getting a filling is quick, comfortable, and usually completed in a single visit. Watch the short walkthrough below, then read what to expect at each stage.

A step-by-step look at how a dental filling repairs a cavity.

1

Examination & Numbing

Dr Agrawal examines the tooth and confirms the extent of the decay, sometimes with an X-ray. The area is then gently numbed with local anaesthesia so you stay comfortable throughout.

For very small cavities, numbing may not even be needed.

2

Removing the Decay

The decayed portion of the tooth is carefully removed, leaving clean, healthy tooth structure behind. This stops the decay from spreading any further.

The area is then cleaned to prepare it for the filling material.

3

Placing the Filling

The tooth-coloured composite is applied in layers and shaped to match the natural contours of your tooth. Each layer is hardened with a special curing light.

The shade is matched to your tooth so the repair blends in seamlessly.

4

Shaping & Polishing

Dr Agrawal checks your bite and fine-tunes the filling so it feels completely natural. The tooth is then polished for a smooth, comfortable finish.

You leave with a fully restored tooth — and in most cases, your treatment is complete in one visit.

Caring for Your Filling

Fillings are low-maintenance, but a few simple habits help them last and keep new cavities away:

  • Wait before eating until any numbness wears off to avoid biting your cheek or tongue.
  • Brush twice a day and floss daily to protect the filled tooth and prevent new decay.
  • Limit sugary and acidic foods that contribute to cavities.
  • Mention any lasting sensitivity or a filling that feels "high" so we can adjust it.
  • Keep up regular checkups so we can monitor your fillings and overall dental health.

Dental Filling FAQs

Does getting a filling hurt?

No. The area is numbed with local anaesthesia, so the procedure is comfortable. Small fillings may not even need numbing. Any mild sensitivity afterwards usually settles within a few days.

How long does a filling take?

Most fillings are completed in a single visit, often within 20 to 40 minutes depending on the size and number of cavities.

How long do fillings last?

With good oral care, tooth-coloured fillings typically last several years, and often much longer. Regular checkups help us monitor them and catch any wear early.

Are tooth-coloured fillings safe?

Yes. Composite fillings are safe, durable, and free of metal. They bond directly to the tooth and are matched to your natural shade for an invisible repair.

Can I eat after getting a filling?

Yes, though it's best to wait until any numbness wears off. Tooth-coloured fillings set immediately, so you can usually eat soon after your appointment.

How do I know if I need a filling?

Common signs include sensitivity, a visible hole or dark spot, or discomfort when eating. Often, small cavities have no symptoms and are found during a routine checkup.
