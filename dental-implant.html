Dental Implants

A permanent, natural-looking way to replace missing teeth — restoring your bite, your smile, and your confidence for years to come.

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. It is placed into the jawbone, where it gradually fuses with the bone to form a strong, permanent foundation for a replacement tooth.

Once the implant has healed, a connector called an abutment is attached to it, and a custom-made crown is fixed on top. The result looks, feels, and functions just like a natural tooth — you can eat, speak, and smile without a second thought.

Implants can replace a single tooth, several teeth, or support a full set of teeth. Because they are anchored in the bone rather than resting on the gums or clasping onto neighbouring teeth, they are widely regarded as the most durable and natural-feeling tooth-replacement option available today.

Why Replacing a Missing Tooth Matters

A missing tooth is about far more than appearance. Left untreated, a single gap can set off a chain of problems that affect your whole mouth over time:

  • Jawbone shrinks Without a tooth root to stimulate it, the bone in that area slowly resorbs, which can change your facial shape and make future treatment harder.
  • Nearby teeth drift Neighbouring teeth gradually tilt or shift into the empty space, disturbing your bite and alignment.
  • Chewing becomes harder Gaps reduce your ability to bite and chew properly, which can affect digestion and food choices.
  • Speech can change Missing front or side teeth can affect how you pronounce certain words.
  • Higher risk of further loss Shifting teeth and extra strain on remaining teeth can lead to more decay, gum problems, and tooth loss down the line.
98% Modern dental implants have a long-term success rate of around 95–98%, making them one of the most reliable and well-studied procedures in dentistry.

Implants vs. a Traditional Bridge

A bridge is a common alternative for replacing a missing tooth, but it works very differently from an implant. Here's how they compare:

Feature Dental Bridge Dental Implant
Adjacent teeth Healthy teeth beside the gap must be filed down to support the bridge Stands on its own — neighbouring teeth are left untouched
Jawbone health Does not stimulate the bone; bone loss continues under the gap Acts like a natural root and helps preserve the jawbone
Longevity Typically 7–10 years before it needs replacing Can last 15+ years, often a lifetime with good care
Feel & function Good, but not fully independent of surrounding teeth Closest option to a natural tooth in look and feel
Upfront cost Lower initial cost Higher upfront, but often better long-term value

A bridge can still be the right choice in some situations. During your consultation, Dr Deepesh Agrawal will help you weigh both options based on your oral health, bone, and budget.

Benefits of Dental Implants

  • Looks and feels natural The crown is colour-matched to your teeth and sits firmly, so it blends in seamlessly.
  • Restores full chewing power Eat the foods you love with confidence — implants restore nearly natural biting strength.
  • Protects your jawbone By stimulating the bone like a real root, implants help prevent bone loss and facial sagging.
  • Keeps your other teeth intact Unlike a bridge, no healthy neighbouring teeth need to be ground down.
  • Built to last With good hygiene and regular checkups, implants can last for decades.
  • Easy to care for No special adhesives or removal — just brush and floss like a natural tooth.

The Implant Procedure, Stage by Stage

Getting a dental implant is a carefully planned process that usually takes place over a few months, mostly to allow natural healing. Watch the short walkthrough below, then read what to expect at each stage.

A step-by-step look at how a dental implant is placed.

1

Consultation & Treatment Planning

Your journey begins with a thorough examination. Dr Agrawal checks the health of your gums and remaining teeth and takes digital X-rays or a 3D scan to assess the quantity and quality of your jawbone.

This planning stage confirms whether you're ready for an implant straight away or whether a preparatory step — such as a tooth extraction or a bone graft to build up thin bone — is needed first. You'll receive a clear, personalised treatment plan and cost estimate before anything begins.

2

Placing the Implant

On the day of surgery, the area is numbed with local anaesthesia so the procedure is comfortable and essentially painless. A small opening is made in the gum, and the titanium implant post is gently placed into the jawbone.

Placing a single implant usually takes under an hour. The gum is then closed over or around the implant, and you'll be given simple aftercare instructions to follow while it heals.

3

Healing & Osseointegration

Over the next few months, a natural process called osseointegration takes place — the jawbone grows around and fuses with the implant, locking it firmly in place.

This healing period typically lasts 3–6 months and is what gives an implant its exceptional strength and stability. In the meantime, a temporary tooth can often be provided so you're never left with a visible gap.

4

Attaching the Abutment

Once the implant has fully integrated, a small connector called an abutment is attached to it. This is a quick, minor step that creates the link between the implant below the gum and the crown that will sit above it.

An impression or scan of the area is then taken so your final crown can be made to fit your bite precisely.

5

Fitting the Crown

Finally, your custom-made crown — shaped and shaded to match your natural teeth — is securely fixed onto the abutment. Dr Agrawal checks that it fits comfortably and that your bite feels even.

You leave with a complete, natural-looking tooth that's ready to use right away. With that, your implant treatment is complete.

Caring for Your Implant

Good aftercare protects your investment and helps your implant last as long as possible. A few simple habits make all the difference:

  • Brush twice a day and floss daily to keep the gum around the implant clean and healthy.
  • Eat soft foods for the first few days after surgery, and avoid chewing directly on the area while it heals.
  • Avoid smoking and tobacco — they slow healing and are a leading cause of implant complications.
  • Take any prescribed medication and use a cold compress if there's mild swelling in the first day or two.
  • Keep up regular checkups and cleanings so we can monitor your implant and surrounding teeth.

Dental Implant FAQs

Does getting a dental implant hurt?

No. The procedure is carried out under local anaesthesia, so you won't feel pain during placement. Most patients report only mild soreness for a day or two afterwards, which is easily managed with regular pain relief.

How long does the whole process take?

Placing the implant takes under an hour, but the implant then needs about 3–6 months to fuse with the jawbone before the final crown is fitted. In some cases faster or single-sitting options may be possible — Dr Agrawal will advise during your consultation.

How long do dental implants last?

With good oral hygiene and routine checkups, implants can last 15+ years and often a lifetime. The crown on top may occasionally need replacing after many years of use.

Am I a suitable candidate for implants?

Most adults with one or more missing teeth and healthy gums are good candidates. Adequate jawbone is needed to support the implant; if bone is limited, a bone graft may be recommended. A quick examination will confirm your suitability.

What if I don't have enough jawbone?

This is common where a tooth has been missing for a while. A bone graft can rebuild the area so it can securely hold an implant. Dr Agrawal will check your bone with a scan and explain your options.

How do I care for my implant?

Just like a natural tooth — brush twice a day, floss daily, and visit us for regular checkups and cleanings. Avoiding smoking is especially important for long-term implant health.
